# Merchant Fraud Policy

## Definition
Merchant fraud occurs when a cardholder is deceived by a fraudulent, fake, or misrepresenting seller into making a payment. Unlike unauthorized transactions (where a third party uses the card without the cardholder's knowledge), merchant fraud involves the cardholder willingly authorizing a payment — but to a merchant who does not intend to deliver what was promised, does not exist, or is operating deceptively. This is the most serious category of dispute and is treated with the highest priority.

## Qualifying Conditions
A dispute qualifies as merchant fraud when:
- The merchant website or storefront was fake and designed solely to collect payments
- The merchant does not exist as a legitimate business entity
- The merchant misrepresented the product or service so significantly that what was received bears no resemblance to what was advertised
- The merchant collected payment with no intention of fulfilling the order
- The merchant's website or contact information disappeared after payment was made
- The merchant is operating a known scam (e.g., fake lottery, fake charity, fake investment, romance scam)
- The merchant used high-pressure tactics, fake reviews, or misleading advertisements to fraudulently induce the purchase
- The cardholder received a counterfeit, empty, or entirely wrong item as part of a deliberate deception (not a shipping error)
- The merchant impersonated a legitimate, well-known brand or retailer

## Detection Signals
The following keywords and phrases in a complaint strongly indicate merchant fraud:
- "The website was fake"
- "The company doesn't exist"
- "It was a complete scam"
- "The seller disappeared after I paid"
- "Fraudulent merchant"
- "They impersonated a real company"
- "Fake store / fake website"
- "It was a scam"
- "They took my money and vanished"
- "The product was completely different from what was advertised"
- "I was deceived into paying"
- "Fake reviews / fake company"
- "Counterfeit goods"

## Distinction from Other Dispute Types
Merchant fraud is distinct from:
- **Goods not received:** In goods not received cases, the merchant is real and may have made an error. In merchant fraud, the merchant is fake or deceptive by design.
- **Unauthorized transaction:** The cardholder did authorize the payment. The problem is that they were fraudulently induced to do so.
- **Billing error:** Billing errors involve legitimate merchants who made a charging mistake. Merchant fraud involves intentional deception.

## Severity Classification
Merchant fraud is classified as the highest severity dispute type due to:
- Intentional deception by a third party
- Potential involvement in organised criminal activity
- Risk of repeat victimisation (the merchant may have other cardholders' data)
- Possible links to money laundering or payment fraud networks

## Resolution Process
1. Cardholder reports the fraudulent merchant with full details
2. Bank immediately flags the merchant in the fraud monitoring system
3. A provisional credit equal to the full disputed amount is issued within 3 business days
4. The bank's fraud investigation team takes over — the cardholder does not need to negotiate with the merchant
5. Bank reports the fraudulent merchant to the card network (Visa/Mastercard fraud registry)
6. A formal chargeback is filed under the "counterfeit/fraudulent merchant" reason code
7. The merchant has 20 days to contest — fraudulent merchants typically cannot provide valid fulfillment evidence
8. Chargeback is upheld and the provisional credit becomes permanent
9. Bank issues a new card with a new number to the cardholder
10. Law enforcement referral is initiated if the fraud amount exceeds $1,000 or if multiple cardholders are affected

## Investigation Timeline
- Provisional credit issued: within 3 business days
- Fraud flag placed on merchant: immediately upon report
- Formal chargeback filed: within 5 business days
- Chargeback resolution: 30–60 days
- Law enforcement referral (if applicable): within 10 business days

## Required Documentation from Customer
- Screenshots of the fraudulent merchant website (URL, product listings, contact page)
- Order confirmation emails or payment receipts
- All communication with the merchant (emails, chat logs, text messages)
- Screenshots of the merchant's social media pages or online listings (if applicable)
- Evidence of misrepresentation (comparison of advertised vs received item, or proof the website disappeared)
- Any correspondence from the merchant that shows deceptive intent
- For counterfeit goods: photographs of the received item compared to the advertised product

## Customer Rights and Obligations
- The cardholder is not liable for any amount lost to merchant fraud
- The cardholder should NOT attempt to return goods to a fraudulent merchant without bank guidance
- The cardholder should preserve all evidence (screenshots, emails, packages) until the investigation is complete
- The cardholder has the right to a new card number immediately upon reporting merchant fraud
- The cardholder should report the merchant to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) at reportfraud.ftc.gov
- The cardholder should also report to the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) at ic3.gov for online fraud
- Filing a dispute does not preclude the cardholder from filing a police report

## Provisional Credit and Card Replacement
Upon verification of a merchant fraud report:
1. A provisional credit for the full disputed amount is applied within 3 business days
2. The existing card is immediately blocked (no further charges can be processed)
3. A replacement card is issued and delivered within 5–7 business days
4. The cardholder's account is reviewed for any other suspicious activity linked to the same merchant

## Common Fraud Schemes and Bank Response
- **Fake online store:** Website mimics a legitimate retailer. Chargeback filed immediately. Merchant reported to card network.
- **Too-good-to-be-true pricing:** Cardholder lured by extreme discounts. Full chargeback upheld — no goods received.
- **Impersonation fraud (fake Apple, Amazon, etc.):** Trademark infringement reported to card network and brand security team.
- **Romance scam / investment scam:** Law enforcement referral initiated. Chargeback filed. Financial counselling offered.
- **Counterfeit luxury goods:** Cardholder advised not to return goods. Full chargeback filed. Customs and law enforcement notified.
- **Subscription trap:** Cardholder signed up for free trial, enrolled in expensive subscription without clear notice. Full refund of fraudulent charges.

## Merchant Blacklisting
Upon confirmation of a fraudulent merchant, the bank:
1. Adds the merchant to the internal fraud merchant list
2. Reports the merchant ID, URL, and payment details to Visa/Mastercard fraud reporting systems
3. Alerts other financial institutions through shared fraud intelligence networks
4. Blocks all future transactions from this merchant for all cardholders
