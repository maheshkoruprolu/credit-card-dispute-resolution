# Billing Error Policy

## Definition
A billing error is any incorrect charge that appears on a cardholder's credit card statement where the amount billed differs from the amount agreed upon, authorized, or expected. Unlike unauthorized transactions (where the cardholder did not authorize any charge), billing errors involve legitimate transactions where the amount, description, or terms are incorrect. This includes overcharges, incorrect fees, interest calculation errors, and charges that do not reflect the agreed price.

## Qualifying Conditions
A dispute qualifies as a billing error when:
- The merchant charged a different amount than what was agreed at the point of sale
- The statement shows a higher amount than the receipt
- The cardholder was charged the wrong price due to a pricing error
- A fee was applied incorrectly (e.g., foreign transaction fee on a domestic purchase)
- Interest was calculated incorrectly on the cardholder's balance
- A credit or refund that was promised by the merchant never appeared on the statement
- The cardholder was charged for a price increase they were not notified about
- A promotional price was not honored and the full price was charged instead
- The cardholder was billed in the wrong currency and the conversion rate was not disclosed

## Detection Signals
The following keywords and phrases in a complaint strongly indicate a billing error:
- "I was overcharged"
- "Wrong amount on my statement"
- "The receipt shows a different amount"
- "They charged me more than the agreed price"
- "Incorrect fee applied"
- "The credit never showed up"
- "My refund was not posted"
- "Interest was calculated incorrectly"
- "The promotional rate was not applied"
- "I was charged the wrong price"

## Distinction from Other Dispute Types
Billing errors are distinct from:
- **Unauthorized transactions:** The cardholder did authorize a transaction but the amount is wrong
- **Duplicate charges:** The same correct amount was charged twice
- **Service not provided:** The cardholder was charged for something they never received at all

## Regulatory Framework
Under the Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA), billing errors must be reported within 60 days of the statement date on which the error first appeared. The bank is required to acknowledge the dispute within 30 days and resolve it within 90 days. During the investigation, the bank cannot report the disputed amount as delinquent or charge interest on it.

## Resolution Process
1. Cardholder submits a written dispute identifying the specific error and the correct amount
2. Bank acknowledges the dispute within 30 days
3. The disputed amount is placed in a temporary hold — no interest accrues during investigation
4. Bank contacts the merchant to verify the correct transaction amount
5. Merchant is asked to provide the signed receipt, invoice, or pricing documentation
6. If the error is confirmed, the bank issues a credit for the difference
7. If the merchant disputes the claim, both parties are notified and may provide additional evidence
8. Final resolution is communicated in writing to the cardholder

## Investigation Timeline
- Acknowledgement: within 30 days of dispute submission
- Merchant response requested: within 5 business days of dispute submission
- Full resolution: within 60–90 days
- Simple cases (clear price discrepancy with receipt): 5–10 business days

## Required Documentation from Customer
- Original receipt or invoice showing the agreed price
- Credit card statement clearly showing the incorrect charge
- Any written or electronic communication from the merchant about pricing (emails, screenshots of website prices, promotional materials)
- Comparison of the agreed amount versus the charged amount
- For refunds not posted: merchant's refund confirmation, return receipt, or email stating refund was issued

## Customer Rights and Obligations
- The cardholder must report billing errors within 60 days of the statement closing date
- The cardholder has the right to withhold payment on the disputed amount without penalty during the investigation
- Interest cannot be charged on the disputed amount while under investigation
- The cardholder must cooperate with the bank's investigation and provide supporting documentation
- The cardholder has the right to request copies of all documentation submitted by the merchant

## Common Scenarios and Outcomes
- **Restaurant added incorrect tip:** Bank requests merchant receipt. If receipt confirms different amount, credit issued for the difference within 5–7 business days.
- **Price at register differed from advertised price:** Cardholder provides screenshot or photo of advertised price. Credit issued for difference after merchant confirmation.
- **Refund never posted:** Bank contacts merchant for refund transaction ID. If confirmed issued, bank posts credit immediately. If merchant failed to issue it, chargeback filed.
- **Incorrect foreign transaction fee:** Fee waived immediately if the cardholder's account type does not include that fee, or if the merchant is domestic.
- **Promotional rate not applied:** Bank requests merchant pricing documentation. Credit issued if promotion is confirmed valid.

## Dispute Amount Calculation
The bank will credit the cardholder for the exact difference between:
- The amount charged on the statement AND
- The amount that should have been charged based on the agreed price, receipt, or promotional terms

The bank does not credit the entire transaction amount unless the entire charge was erroneous.
