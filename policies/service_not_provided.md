# Service Not Provided Policy

## Definition
A "Service Not Provided" dispute arises when a cardholder pays for a service — such as a subscription, membership, repair, professional service, event ticket, travel booking, or any time-based service — and that service is either never rendered, is cancelled by the provider without a refund, or continues to be charged after the cardholder has cancelled. Unlike goods disputes (which involve physical or digital products), this policy specifically covers services that were paid for but not delivered.

## Qualifying Conditions
A dispute qualifies as service not provided when:
- A subscription or membership was cancelled by the cardholder, but charges continued after the cancellation date
- The cardholder paid for a service in advance and the provider failed to deliver it
- An appointment, booking, or reservation was confirmed but the service was never performed
- An event (concert, flight, hotel stay, class) was cancelled by the organizer and no refund was issued
- A contractor or service professional was paid upfront but did not complete the work
- A gym, streaming service, or software platform charged the cardholder after the cancellation was confirmed
- Travel services (hotel, airline, car rental) were paid for but the booking was cancelled by the provider without a refund
- A free trial was converted to a paid subscription without adequate notice to the cardholder

## Detection Signals
The following keywords and phrases in a complaint strongly indicate service not provided:
- "I cancelled my membership but was still charged"
- "They kept charging me after I cancelled"
- "Service was never performed"
- "Contractor never showed up"
- "Event was cancelled and I never got a refund"
- "Hotel charged me but cancelled my booking"
- "Flight was cancelled and no refund was given"
- "I cancelled the subscription but charges continued"
- "Charged for a service I never received"
- "Free trial converted to paid without my knowledge"
- "I was charged after cancellation"

## Distinction from Other Dispute Types
Service not provided is distinct from:
- **Goods not received:** This policy applies specifically to services, not physical or digital products
- **Unauthorized transaction:** The cardholder authorized the original service payment. The problem is that the service was not delivered or continued after cancellation.
- **Billing error:** The amount is correct but the service itself was not rendered. If the service was rendered but the wrong amount was charged, that is a billing error.

## Categories of Service Disputes

### Subscription and Membership Cancellations
When a cardholder cancels a recurring subscription or membership:
- The cancellation date is the cut-off for future charges
- Any charge processed after the confirmed cancellation date is fully disputable
- The cardholder must provide confirmation of cancellation (email, screenshot, confirmation number)
- Charges processed on the same day as cancellation may be subject to the provider's billing cycle policy

### Advance Payment for Services Not Rendered
When a cardholder pays in advance for a service that is never performed:
- The full amount paid is disputable
- The cardholder must demonstrate the service was not performed (no appointment, no work completed, no event attended)
- A reasonable waiting period (5–10 business days past the scheduled service date) is expected before filing a dispute

### Provider-Cancelled Events and Bookings
When a service provider cancels an event, booking, or appointment:
- The cardholder is entitled to a full refund
- If the refund is not issued within 10 business days of cancellation, a dispute may be filed
- The bank will request evidence of the cancellation from the provider

### Free Trial Conversions
If a service converted a free trial to a paid subscription without adequate notice:
- Cardholder must provide evidence that no notification was received or that the notification was deceptive
- Bank will review the terms of the original sign-up and assess whether proper notice was given
- If proper notice was not given, the charge is disputable

## Resolution Process
1. Cardholder reports the unrendered service and provides the cancellation or service date
2. Bank advises the cardholder to contact the merchant to request a refund (required first step)
3. If the merchant does not resolve the issue within 5 business days, the bank opens a formal dispute
4. Bank contacts the merchant requesting proof that the service was rendered (service records, appointment logs, delivery confirmation)
5. Merchant must also provide cancellation policy and any signed agreements
6. If the merchant cannot prove the service was rendered, or if charges occurred after confirmed cancellation, a chargeback is filed
7. Credit is applied to the cardholder's account
8. Resolution is communicated in writing

## Investigation Timeline
- Initial review: 1–2 business days
- Cardholder-merchant resolution attempt: 5 business days recommended
- Merchant response to bank dispute: 10 business days
- Credit issued (post-cancellation charges): 5–7 business days after verification
- Credit issued (service never rendered): 10–20 business days
- Full chargeback resolution: 30–60 days

## Required Documentation from Customer
- **For cancelled subscriptions:** Cancellation confirmation email, screenshot of cancellation confirmation page, or cancellation reference number
- **For services not rendered:** Appointment or booking confirmation, evidence that no service was provided (no contractor visit, no event held, no appointment kept)
- **For cancelled events/travel:** Cancellation notice from the provider (email, SMS, or website notification)
- **For free trial conversions:** Evidence of original sign-up terms, proof that no advance notice of billing was received

## Customer Rights and Obligations
- The cardholder has the right to cancel any subscription at any time and stop future charges
- Post-cancellation charges are fully recoverable regardless of the merchant's internal billing cycle
- The cardholder must retain proof of cancellation indefinitely (at least until the final billing cycle has cleared)
- Disputes must be filed within 120 days of the charge date
- The cardholder must cooperate with the bank's information requests within 10 business days

## Post-Cancellation Charge Protection
Once a cancellation is confirmed, the bank will:
1. Credit all charges that occurred after the confirmed cancellation date
2. Flag the merchant for monitoring in case charges recur
3. Optionally block the merchant from charging the account again (upon cardholder request)
4. Assist the cardholder in cancelling any stored payment methods with the merchant

## Common Scenarios and Outcomes
- **Gym membership cancelled, charges continued:** Bank credits all post-cancellation charges within 5–7 business days after reviewing cancellation confirmation.
- **Streaming service charged after cancellation:** Immediate credit issued upon confirmation of cancellation date. No merchant contact needed for amounts under $50.
- **Contractor paid upfront, never completed work:** Dispute opened. Merchant has 10 business days to provide proof of completion. If none provided, full chargeback issued.
- **Flight cancelled, no refund issued:** Bank contacts airline for cancellation and refund documentation. If airline confirms cancellation but did not refund, chargeback filed.
- **Hotel charged for cancelled booking:** If cancellation was within the hotel's free-cancellation window, credit issued within 10 business days.
