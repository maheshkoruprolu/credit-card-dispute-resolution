# Duplicate Charge Policy

## Definition
A duplicate charge occurs when a cardholder is charged more than once for the same single transaction. Both charges are identical or nearly identical in amount, merchant name, and date, but only one charge was authorized by the cardholder. Duplicate charges most commonly arise from merchant processing errors, double-swiping at point of sale, payment gateway timeouts causing the system to resubmit the transaction, or technical errors in the merchant's billing system.

## Qualifying Conditions
A dispute qualifies as a duplicate charge when:
- The same transaction amount from the same merchant appears two or more times on the statement
- The cardholder made one purchase but the statement reflects multiple identical or near-identical charges
- The duplicate charge occurred on the same day or within 24–48 hours of the original
- A subscription or recurring charge was processed multiple times in the same billing cycle
- A payment that was previously declined was resubmitted and charged after the cardholder used an alternate payment method
- The merchant's terminal produced two receipts for a single tap/swipe

## Detection Signals
The following keywords and phrases in a complaint strongly indicate a duplicate charge:
- "Charged twice for the same purchase"
- "Double charged"
- "Two identical charges from the same merchant"
- "Same transaction appeared twice on my statement"
- "Billed twice in the same day"
- "Duplicate transaction"
- "I only made one purchase but see two charges"
- "They ran my card twice by mistake"

## Distinction from Other Dispute Types
Duplicate charges are distinct from:
- **Unauthorized transactions:** At least one of the duplicate charges was authorized. The second (or additional) charge is the problem.
- **Billing errors:** The amount charged is correct but the number of times charged is wrong
- **Service not provided:** This applies when nothing was received. Duplicate charges assume the service/goods were received once.

## Common Causes of Duplicate Charges
1. **Point-of-sale terminal errors:** Card reader malfunction causes double processing
2. **Payment timeout:** Network delay causes the payment system to resubmit a transaction believing the first failed
3. **Manual re-entry:** Cashier enters the transaction manually after a card swipe appears to fail
4. **E-commerce errors:** Website bug causes order to be submitted twice; both trigger charges
5. **Subscription system errors:** Recurring billing software processes the same period twice
6. **Refund processing error:** A refund is processed as another charge instead

## Resolution Process
1. Cardholder identifies and reports the duplicate charge with both transaction dates and amounts
2. Bank verifies both charges appear on the account with matching merchant, amount, and date
3. Bank contacts the merchant to confirm whether one or both transactions are legitimate
4. Merchant typically confirms the error and issues a refund within 3–5 business days
5. If the merchant does not respond within 5 business days, the bank initiates a chargeback for the duplicate amount
6. Credit for the duplicate amount is applied to the cardholder's account
7. Cardholder receives written confirmation of the resolution

## Investigation Timeline
- Verification of duplicate charges: within 1–2 business days
- Merchant contact and response: 3–5 business days
- Credit issued (if merchant confirms): 3–5 business days from merchant confirmation
- Chargeback initiated (if merchant unresponsive): after 5 business days
- Full chargeback resolution: 30–45 days

## Required Documentation from Customer
- Credit card statement showing both identical or near-identical charges
- Receipt or order confirmation for the original legitimate transaction (to distinguish which charge was authorized)
- Any merchant communication acknowledging the error (email, text message, or chat transcript)
- For online purchases: order confirmation email and screenshot of the order history showing a single order

## Customer Rights and Obligations
- The cardholder is only responsible for one charge per transaction
- The bank will not charge interest on the duplicate amount during investigation
- The cardholder should attempt to contact the merchant directly first, as many merchants resolve duplicates immediately
- If the merchant resolves the issue directly, the cardholder should inform the bank to close the dispute
- The cardholder must provide documentation to confirm which charge is the legitimate one

## What the Bank Does Not Credit
The bank will only credit the duplicate portion — one of the two (or more) identical charges. The original, legitimate charge remains on the statement and is the cardholder's responsibility.

## Merchant Response Scenarios
- **Merchant confirms duplicate and refunds:** Credit appears on statement within 3–7 business days. No chargeback needed.
- **Merchant claims both charges are for separate orders:** Bank requests proof of two separate orders (two invoices, two order numbers, two delivery confirmations). If merchant cannot provide this, chargeback is upheld.
- **Merchant is unresponsive:** Chargeback filed after 5 business days. Duplicate charge reversed within 30–45 days.

## Subscription Duplicate Charges
If a duplicate occurs in a recurring billing context (subscriptions, memberships, utility autopay), the bank will:
1. Credit the duplicate amount immediately upon verification
2. Notify the merchant of the billing error
3. Monitor the account for one full billing cycle to ensure the error does not recur
4. If the duplicate recurs, escalate to the merchant's billing department and request a billing correction
