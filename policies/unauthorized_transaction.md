# Unauthorized Transaction Policy

## Definition
An unauthorized transaction occurs when a credit card charge is made without the cardholder's knowledge, consent, or permission. This includes transactions made by a third party who obtained the card details through theft, skimming, phishing, data breach, or account takeover. Transactions made by a family member or acquaintance without explicit permission also qualify as unauthorized under this policy.

## Qualifying Conditions
A dispute qualifies as an unauthorized transaction when the cardholder:
- Did not make, initiate, or authorize the purchase in any form
- Has no relationship with the merchant where the charge originated
- Reports that their physical card was lost or stolen
- Suspects their card number was stolen without the physical card being taken
- Identifies charges made after reporting the card lost or stolen
- Discovers multiple unfamiliar charges in a short time window, suggesting card compromise
- Receives notification of a transaction they did not perform

## Detection Signals
The following keywords and phrases in a complaint strongly indicate an unauthorized transaction:
- "I did not make this purchase"
- "I never authorized this charge"
- "Someone used my card without my permission"
- "My card was stolen / lost"
- "I don't recognize this merchant"
- "Fraudulent charge on my account"
- "Identity theft"
- "Account was hacked"
- "Card skimmed"

## Regulatory Framework
Under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) and the Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA), cardholders are protected against unauthorized use. The bank is legally obligated to investigate all reported unauthorized transactions. Maximum cardholder liability is $50 if reported promptly. For card-not-present fraud (online purchases), cardholder liability is typically $0 under Visa, Mastercard, and major network zero-liability policies.

## Resolution Process
1. Cardholder reports the unauthorized transaction to the bank
2. Bank immediately flags the transaction for investigation
3. A provisional (temporary) credit is issued to the cardholder's account within 5 business days
4. The card is blocked and a replacement card is issued within 5–7 business days
5. The fraud investigation team contacts the merchant and payment processor
6. The chargeback is formally filed with the card network (Visa/Mastercard)
7. Merchant has 20 days to contest the chargeback with evidence
8. If merchant cannot prove authorization, the chargeback is upheld and credit becomes permanent
9. If merchant provides signed proof of authorization, the provisional credit is reversed and cardholder is notified

## Investigation Timeline
- Provisional credit issued: within 5 business days of report
- Initial investigation completed: within 10 business days
- Full resolution: within 45–90 days (depending on merchant response)
- Expedited review (amounts over $500): within 3–5 business days

## Required Documentation from Customer
- Government-issued photo ID
- Written statement describing the unauthorized transaction
- Credit card statement highlighting the disputed charge
- Police report (mandatory if transaction amount exceeds $500)
- Any relevant evidence: phishing emails, suspicious text messages, ATM photos

## Customer Rights and Obligations
- The cardholder must report the unauthorized transaction within 60 days of the statement date to maintain full protection
- Reporting within 2 business days limits liability to $50
- Reporting between 2–60 days limits liability to $500
- Reporting after 60 days may result in full liability for losses
- The cardholder has the right to a written explanation of the investigation outcome
- The cardholder may request copies of all evidence used in the investigation

## Provisional Credit Policy
A provisional credit equal to the disputed amount is applied to the cardholder's account while the investigation is ongoing. This credit is temporary and may be reversed if the investigation concludes in the merchant's favor. The cardholder will receive written notice 5 business days before any provisional credit is reversed, allowing time to provide additional evidence.

## Common Scenarios and Outcomes
- **Card physically stolen and used:** Chargeback upheld in 95% of cases. Provisional credit issued immediately.
- **Card number stolen online (card present):** Chargeback upheld in 90% of cases. Merchant must prove card was physically present.
- **Account takeover:** Full investigation initiated. All unauthorized charges reversed. Account security review conducted.
- **Family member used card without permission:** Cardholder must submit a signed declaration. Chargeback process is the same but may require additional verification.
